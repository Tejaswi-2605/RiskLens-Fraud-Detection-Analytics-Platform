==========================================================================
  FITTING CALIBRATORS (isotonic vs Platt)
==========================================================================
ISOTONIC   any non-decreasing step function. Flexible, needs data.
PLATT      a logistic curve - two parameters. Cannot overfit, but
           can only apply an S-shaped correction.

Selection happens on val_late, NOT on the data they were fitted to -
judging a fitted transform on its own training data would always
favour the more flexible one, which is the overfitting we want to
detect.

==========================================================================
  SANITY CHECK - did the calibrator reorder anything?
==========================================================================
The right test is INVERSIONS, not 'PR-AUC must be identical'.

An earlier version of this script checked PR-AUC equality and
reported a spurious FAIL on a perfectly correct isotonic
calibrator. Isotonic is a step function: it never reorders, but it
creates enormous numbers of TIES, and average_precision treats tied
scores differently from distinct ones. ROC-AUC barely moves because
it averages over ties.

So the honest check is: sort by raw score, and confirm the
calibrated score never DECREASES. Equal is fine; lower is a real
inversion.
